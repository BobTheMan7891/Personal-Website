Computer Science NEA

This project is a significant piece of coursework as part of my A-Level Computer Science course. It challenged me to put my theoretical knowledge, learned from the A-Level course, into practice. My project tackled the issue of a lack of offline chess games that offer multiple chess variants and was accompanied by a document that went over the development process. This document included sections on initial planning, design, implementation and testing, which helped me sharpen my software design, problem-solving and programming skills.

One challenge that I overcome as part of this project was implementing a login and sign-up system, including a local databse and password hashing. This was because the skills required for this went beyond my knowledge at the time. To resolve the issue I pushed myself to research and learn knew concepts independently, giving me a solid understanding of core security principals in software development.

Exam board: AQA

Grade: A


EPQ

As optional, additional coursework during my A-Level study period, I completed an Extended Project Qualification (EPQ), producing an artefact that explores the evidence-based advantages of puzzle games. This involved researching studies on the cognitive and educational advantages/disadvantages of puzzle-based video games to better understand how they can support skills such as problem-solving. This research then applied to the development of my own puzzle game.

This project strengthened my research skills, as I was required to evaluate numerous academic sources of information to construct a balanced argument of my own. Since the EPQ is to be completed independently, I developed valuable skills in project management as I planned my research and development process in order to meet deadlines. This was all done while balancing my A-Level workload.

Exam board: AQA

Grade: A*
